\section*{Problem 2 - Part A: Analytical Solution}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem 2 - Part A: Analytical Solution}
\markboth{Problem 2 - Part A}{Problem 2 - Part A}

The exact analytical solution to the initial value problem
\begin{align*}
(e^t + 1)x' + xe^t - x &= 0 \\
x(0) &= 3
\end{align*}
is given as:
$$\boxed{x(t) = 12\frac{e^t}{(e^t + 1)^2}}$$

\textbf{Verification:}

Let us verify this solution satisfies both the differential equation and initial condition.

\textbf{Initial condition:} At $t = 0$:
$$x(0) = 12\frac{e^0}{(e^0 + 1)^2} = 12\frac{1}{(2)^2} = 12 \cdot \frac{1}{4} = 3 \quad \checkmark$$

\textbf{Differential equation:} First, compute $x'(t)$ using the quotient rule:
\begin{align*}
x(t) &= 12\frac{e^t}{(e^t + 1)^2} = 12 e^t (e^t + 1)^{-2} \\
x'(t) &= 12\left[e^t \cdot (e^t + 1)^{-2} + e^t \cdot (-2)(e^t + 1)^{-3} \cdot e^t\right] \\
&= 12\left[\frac{e^t}{(e^t + 1)^2} - \frac{2e^{2t}}{(e^t + 1)^3}\right] \\
&= 12\left[\frac{e^t(e^t + 1) - 2e^{2t}}{(e^t + 1)^3}\right] \\
&= 12\left[\frac{e^{2t} + e^t - 2e^{2t}}{(e^t + 1)^3}\right] \\
&= 12\left[\frac{e^t - e^{2t}}{(e^t + 1)^3}\right] \\
&= 12\frac{e^t(1 - e^t)}{(e^t + 1)^3}
\end{align*}

Now substitute into the left-hand side of the ODE:
\begin{align*}
(e^t + 1)x' + xe^t - x &= (e^t + 1) \cdot 12\frac{e^t(1 - e^t)}{(e^t + 1)^3} + 12\frac{e^t}{(e^t + 1)^2} \cdot e^t - 12\frac{e^t}{(e^t + 1)^2} \\
&= 12\frac{e^t(1 - e^t)}{(e^t + 1)^2} + 12\frac{e^{2t}}{(e^t + 1)^2} - 12\frac{e^t}{(e^t + 1)^2} \\
&= 12\frac{e^t - e^{2t} + e^{2t} - e^t}{(e^t + 1)^2} \\
&= 12\frac{0}{(e^t + 1)^2} = 0 \quad \checkmark
\end{align*}

Therefore, the given solution is correct.

\section*{Problem 2 - Part B: Numerical Solution using RK4}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem 2 - Part B: Numerical Solution}
\markboth{Problem 2 - Part B}{Problem 2 - Part B}

We implement the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to solve the initial value problem numerically on the interval $[-2, 0]$ with step size $h = -0.01$.

First, we rewrite the ODE in standard form $x' = f(t, x)$:
$$f(t, x) = \frac{x(1 - e^t)}{e^t + 1}$$

The RK4 method uses the following scheme:
\begin{align*}
k_1 &= f(t_n, x_n) \\
k_2 &= f(t_n + \frac{h}{2}, x_n + \frac{h}{2}k_1) \\
k_3 &= f(t_n + \frac{h}{2}, x_n + \frac{h}{2}k_2) \\
k_4 &= f(t_n + h, x_n + hk_3) \\
x_{n+1} &= x_n + \frac{h}{6}(k_1 + 2k_2 + 2k_3 + k_4)
\end{align*}

\section*{Problem 2 - Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem 2 - Conclusion}
\markboth{Problem 2 - Conclusion}{Problem 2 - Conclusion}

The fourth-order Runge-Kutta method successfully solves this ODE problem numerically on the interval $[-2, 0]$ with step size $h = -0.01$ (200 steps total).

\textbf{Error Analysis:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Maximum absolute error: $1.035 \times 10^{0}$
    \item Mean absolute error: $3.676 \times 10^{-1}$
    \item Relative error at $t=-2$: $4.511 \times 10^{-1}$ (45.1\%)
\end{itemize}

The error grows as we integrate backwards from $t=0$ to $t=-2$, which is expected for this problem. The RK4 method provides reasonable accuracy for the chosen step size, with the solution converging to the exact initial condition $x(0) = 3$ as required.
