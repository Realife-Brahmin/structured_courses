\textbf{Problem Statement:} Use Taylor theorem to derive the error term for the approximate formula
$$f'(x) \approx \frac{1}{2h} (-3f(x) + 4f(x+h) - f(x+2h)).$$

\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Solution:}

We are given the finite-difference formula:
\begin{equation*}
    f'(x) \approx \frac{1}{2h}\left[-3f(x) + 4f(x+h) - f(x+2h)\right]
\end{equation*}

Expand $f(x+h)$ and $f(x+2h)$ about $x$ using Taylor's theorem, keeping terms up to $h^2$ and representing higher-order terms as $k_1 h^3$ and $k_2 h^4$:
\begin{align*}
    f(x+h) &= f(x) + h f'(x) + \frac{h^2}{2} f''(x) + k_1 h^3 \\
    f(x+2h) &= f(x) + 2h f'(x) + 2h^2 f''(x) + k_2 h^3
\end{align*}
where $k_1, k_2$ are finite constants depending on third order derivatives of $f$ at $x$.

Substitute into the formula:
\begin{align*}
    &\frac{1}{2h}\left[-3f(x) + 4f(x+h) - f(x+2h)\right] \\
    &= \frac{1}{2h}\Big[-3f(x) + 4\left(f(x) + h f'(x) + \frac{h^2}{2} f''(x) + k_1 h^3\right) \\
    &\qquad - \left(f(x) + 2h f'(x) + 2h^2 f''(x) + k_2 h^3\right)\Big]
\end{align*}

Simplifying the terms in the square brackets:
\begin{align*}
    &= \frac{1}{2h}\Big[2h f'(x) + (4k_1 - k_2) h^3\Big]
\end{align*}

Now, divide each term by $2h$:
\begin{align*}
    &= f'(x) + \frac{1}{2} (4k_1 - k_2) h^2
\end{align*}

Thus, the leading error term is $O(h^2)$:
\begin{equation*}
    \text{error} \sim O(h^2)
\end{equation*}

This shows the method is second-order accurate. Note that I've already checked if the third order term vanishes if we write out the taylor's polynomial to the third order, and it does not.
