On the left-hand side of the switch/breaker, we have the source voltage and a resistance and inductance representing the supply system combined with the transformer. The capacitor to the left of the breaker represents the combined substation capacitance due to the substation busbars. On the right-hand side of the breaker, we have the transmission line parameters: resistance, inductance, and capacitance represented as simple lumped parameters, where the right side is short-circuited due to the fault, assuming that the fault has already been applied. We have assumed a single-phase circuit here for simplicity. The parameters of the circuit below are: $e(t) = 230\times\cos(\omega t)$ kV, $\omega=377$ rad/s, $R_1=3\Omega$, $L_1=350$ mH, $R_2=50\Omega$, $L_2=100$ mH, $C_1=10$ nF, $C_2=600$ nF. Assume that the system is operating with zero initial conditions and the breaker is initially closed. At $t=8$ ms, the breaker is commanded to open the line, but it will not actually open until the current passes through zero. The study period is from $t=0$ to $t=20$ ms.

1) Choose an appropriate simulation time-step size $\Delta t$ (assuming the Trapezoidal integration rule) according to the time constants in the circuit. For this, evaluate the approximate resonant frequencies analytically before and after the breaker operation.

\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Solution:}

\subsection{Solution Approach}

To determine an appropriate time-step for the trapezoidal discretization, we need to analyze the resonant frequencies of the circuit in three different states:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Closed state}: Breaker closed (full circuit)
    \item \textbf{Open state (Left)}: Left portion of the circuit after breaker opens
    \item \textbf{Open state (Right)}: Right portion of the circuit after breaker opens
\end{enumerate}

The circuit configurations are shown in Figures~\ref{fig:c01_closed_handdrawn} and \ref{fig:c01_open_handdrawn}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/figure-C-closed-circuit-handdrawn.png}
    \caption{Closed circuit configuration (breaker closed)}
    \label{fig:c01_closed_handdrawn}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]

    \caption{Open circuit configuration (breaker open, showing left and right portions)}

\end{figure}

The criterion for time-step selection is based on the maximum frequency component in the circuit to ensure numerical accuracy with the trapezoidal rule.

\subsection{Resonant Frequency Calculations}

For an LC circuit, the resonant frequency is given by:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\text{res}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Closed State (Breaker Closed)}

When the breaker is closed, the inductances are in parallel and capacitances are in series:
\begin{align}
    L_{\text{eq}} &= \frac{L_1 L_2}{L_1 + L_2} = \frac{(0.350)(0.100)}{0.350 + 0.100} = 0.0778\text{ H} \\
    C_{\text{eq}} &= C_1 + C_2 = 10\times 10^{-9} + 600\times 10^{-9} = 6.10\times 10^{-7}\text{ F}
\end{align}

The resonant frequency is:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\text{res,closed}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(0.0778)(6.10\times 10^{-7})}} = 730.68\text{ Hz}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Open State - Left Portion}

When the breaker is open, the left portion consists of $L_1$ and $C_1$:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\text{res,left}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L_1 C_1}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(0.350)(10\times 10^{-9})}} = 2690.21\text{ Hz}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Open State - Right Portion}

The right portion consists of $L_2$ and $C_2$:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\text{res,right}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L_2 C_2}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(0.100)(600\times 10^{-9})}} = 649.75\text{ Hz}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Time-Step Selection Criterion}

For the trapezoidal rule with 3\% error tolerance, the recommended time-step is:
\begin{equation}
    h = \frac{1}{10 f_{\max}}
\end{equation}

where $f_{\max} = f_{\text{res}}$ for each circuit state.

\subsection{Recommended Time-Steps}

Computing the time-step for each state:

\begin{align}
    h_{\text{closed}} &= \frac{1}{10 \times 730.68} = 1.369\times 10^{-4}\text{ s} = 136.86\,\mu\text{s} \\
    h_{\text{left}} &= \frac{1}{10 \times 2690.21} = 3.717\times 10^{-5}\text{ s} = 37.17\,\mu\text{s} \\
    h_{\text{right}} &= \frac{1}{10 \times 649.75} = 1.539\times 10^{-4}\text{ s} = 153.91\,\mu\text{s}
\end{align}

\subsection{Overall Recommendation}

To ensure accurate simulation throughout all circuit states (before and after breaker operation), we must use the most conservative (minimum) time-step:

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!5!white, colframe=blue!75!black, title=\textbf{Simulation Time-Step Recommendation}]
\centering
\Large
$\boxed{h = 37.17\,\mu\text{s}}$

\vspace{0.3em}
\normalsize
This corresponds to the open state (left portion), which has the highest resonant frequency.
\end{tcolorbox}
