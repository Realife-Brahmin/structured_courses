\textbf{Observations:}

\textbf{Inductor Voltage:} The inductor voltage $v_L(t)$ exhibits the expected exponential decay from an initial value of approximately 10~V (at $t = 0^+$) to zero as the circuit reaches steady state. The voltage decays with time constant $\tau = L/R = 2$~ms. Both time-step sizes capture this behavior accurately, with the finer time step ($\Delta t = 0.1$~ms) providing smoother resolution of the transient. The coarser time step ($\Delta t = 0.8$~ms) still captures the overall decay trend but with fewer sample points.

\textbf{Inductor Current:} The inductor current $i(t)$ rises exponentially from zero to its steady-state value of $I_{\text{ss}} = V_{\text{DC}}/R = 1$~A, following the complementary behavior to the voltage. Both time-step sizes converge to the same steady-state value, demonstrating that PSCAD's trapezoidal integration method is stable for this problem. The finer time step provides better resolution of the initial transient behavior.

\textbf{Step Size Impact:} The coarser time step ($\Delta t = 0.8$~ms) is close to half the time constant ($\tau/2.5 = 0.8$~ms), yet still produces acceptable results without numerical instability. This demonstrates the robustness of PSCAD's implicit integration scheme for this first-order RL circuit.
