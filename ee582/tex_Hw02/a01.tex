\section*{Section A (Numerical Oscillations of Discretization Rules): Question 1}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Section A (Numerical Oscillations of Discretization Rules): Question 1}

One way to analyze the properties of various discretization methods (e.g., in terms of their numerical oscillations) is to solve a lossless first-order system (e.g., inductance or capacitance) when the state variable of the component is suddenly changed. The circuit on the right considers a case where a dc voltage is suddenly applied to a capacitor. Because the source voltage is applied like a step function, we know that the current of the capacitor should look like an impulse, analytically. Let us assume that in the simulations all the variables are zero at $t=0$ and the voltage source takes its value at $t=\Delta t$.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.4\textwidth]{figures/figure-A-circuit.png}
    \caption{Simple capacitor circuit with DC voltage source}
    \label{fig:a01_circuit}
\end{figure}

1) Determine the step-by-step solution for the current in the capacitor (in terms of capacitance $C$, time-step size $\Delta t$, and source voltage) using the following discretization rules: a) Forward Euler, b) Backward Euler, c) Trapezoidal.

\vspace{1em}

\subsection*{Trapezoidal Discretization of Capacitor}
The equations for the Trapezoidal discretization of a capacitor are (see Appendix \ref{sec:appendix_cap_trapz} for derivation):

The final equations for $v(t)$ and $i(t)$ are as follows:

\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{v(t) &= e_h + R_C i(t)} \label{eq:vt_cap_trapz_app} \\
    \Aboxed{i(t) &= \frac{v(t)}{R_C} - i_h} \label{eq:it_cap_trapz_app}
\end{align}

where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $R_C = \frac{\Delta t}{2C}$ is the equivalent resistance.
    \item $e_h = v(t - \Delta t) + R_C i(t - \Delta t)$ is the history voltage.
    \item $i_h = \frac{v(t - \Delta t)}{R_C} - i(t - \Delta t)$ is the history current.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Backward-Euler (BE) Discretization of Capacitor}
The equations for the Backward-Euler discretization of a capacitor are (see Appendix \ref{sec:appendix_cap_be} for derivation):

\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{v(t) &= e_h + R_C\, i(t)} \label{eq:vt_cap_be_cap} \\
    \Aboxed{i(t) &= \frac{v(t)}{R_C} - i_h} \label{eq:it_cap_be_cap}
\end{align}

where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $R_C = \dfrac{\Delta t}{C}$
    \item $e_h = v(t-\Delta t) + R_C i(t-\Delta t)$
    \item $i_h = \dfrac{v(t-\Delta t)}{R_C}$
\end{itemize}
