\section*{Problem 8}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem 8}
\markboth{Problem 8}{Problem 8}

\textbf{Problem Statement (Textbook 6.1.1d):} 

Find the polynomial of least degree that interpolates these sets of data:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
$x$ & 3 & 7 & 12 \\
$y$ & 12 & 146 & 2
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Solution:}

We find the polynomial of least degree interpolating the data points $(3, 12)$, $(7, 146)$, and $(12, 2)$.

Since we have 3 data points, the interpolating polynomial has degree at most $n-1 = 2$ (quadratic).

\textbf{Method: Vandermonde Matrix}

We seek a polynomial $P(x) = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2$ such that $P(x_i) = y_i$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$.

This gives the linear system (see Textbook p. 313, Equation 12) \cite{kincaid2009numerical}:
$$\begin{bmatrix} 
1 & x_1 & x_1^2 \\
1 & x_2 & x_2^2 \\
1 & x_3 & x_3^2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} a_0 \\ a_1 \\ a_2 \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \\ y_3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Substituting our data:
$$\begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 3 & 9 \\
1 & 7 & 49 \\
1 & 12 & 144
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} a_0 \\ a_1 \\ a_2 \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} 12 \\ 146 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Solving this system, we obtain:
\begin{align*}
a_0 &= -\frac{4211}{18} \approx -233.8667 \\
a_1 &= \frac{1849}{18} \approx 102.7222 \\
a_2 &= -\frac{125}{18} \approx -6.9222
\end{align*}

\textbf{Implementation:} See Appendix B (\ref{app:p08-vandermonde}) for Julia code implementation.

\textbf{Final Answer:}

$$\boxed{P(x) = -\frac{125}{18}x^2 + \frac{1849}{18}x - \frac{4211}{18}}$$

Or in decimal form:
$$\boxed{P(x) = -6.9222x^2 + 102.7222x - 233.8667}$$

\textbf{Verification:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item $P(3) = 12.000$
    \item $P(7) = 146.000$
    \item $P(12) = 2.000$
\end{itemize}

Maximum interpolation error: $5.68 \times 10^{-14}$ (numerical precision).
